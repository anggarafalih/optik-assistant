# Blue-Light Filtering Lenses

Blue-light filtering lenses use a coating or a lens material that reflects or absorbs part of the high-energy blue-violet light, roughly 400 to 455 nanometres. Coated versions usually show a faint blue reflection on the lens surface. Some versions have a slight yellow tint.

## Where blue light comes from

The sun is by far the largest source of blue light. Phone, computer, and TV screens and LED lighting emit much smaller amounts.

## What the evidence says

Current research does not show that blue-light filtering lenses reduce digital eye strain. A 2023 Cochrane systematic review found little or no short-term benefit for eye strain, and the American Academy of Ophthalmology does not recommend blue-light glasses for computer use. Light from screens is not known to damage the eyes.

Digital eye strain is mainly caused by blinking less while looking at a screen, focusing at a near distance for a long time, glare, and uncorrected vision problems. What helps more:

- Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet (about 6 metres) away for 20 seconds.
- Wear an up-to-date prescription.
- Choose an anti-reflective coating to reduce glare.
- Position the screen at a comfortable distance and height.

Evening exposure to bright screens may affect sleep, but evidence that blue-light glasses improve sleep is mixed. Reducing screen time before bed or using the device's night mode are simple alternatives.

## Who might still choose them

Blue-light filtering lenses are harmless, and some customers simply prefer them. They are usually combined with an anti-reflective coating and can be added to single vision or progressive lenses. Our staff will explain the evidence honestly so customers can make an informed choice.
