# Lens Materials and Refractive Index

The refractive index of a lens material describes how strongly it bends light. A higher index bends light more, so a lens with a higher index can be thinner and lighter for the same prescription.

## Common materials

| Material | Index | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CR-39 (standard plastic) | 1.50 | Excellent optical clarity, affordable. Thicker for strong prescriptions and less impact resistant. Needs a UV coating. |
| Polycarbonate | 1.59 | Very impact resistant, lightweight, built-in UV protection. Recommended for children, sports, and safety glasses. Lower optical clarity at the edges for some wearers (colour fringes). |
| Trivex | 1.53 | Impact resistant like polycarbonate, with better optical clarity. Very light. |
| High-index 1.60 | 1.60 | Thinner than standard plastic, good for moderate prescriptions. |
| High-index 1.67 | 1.67 | Thin, for strong prescriptions. |
| High-index 1.74 | 1.74 | The thinnest plastic lens, for very strong prescriptions. |
| Glass | 1.52 and up | Rarely used today. Scratch resistant but heavy and can shatter. |

## Which index for which prescription

As a general guide, based on the strongest power in the prescription (sphere plus cylinder):

- Up to ±2.00: standard 1.50 plastic or polycarbonate.
- ±2.25 to ±4.00: 1.60.
- ±4.25 to ±6.00: 1.67.
- Stronger than ±6.00: 1.74.

This is only a guide. Frame size and shape matter too. For minus (nearsighted) prescriptions the edges of the lens are thicker, and for plus (farsighted) prescriptions the centre is thicker. Smaller frames reduce lens thickness.

## Rimless and semi-rimless frames

Rimless frames hold the lens with screws through drilled holes. Polycarbonate, Trivex, or high-index lenses are recommended for rimless frames, because standard CR-39 plastic is more likely to crack or chip around the drill holes.
