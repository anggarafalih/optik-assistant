# Contact Lens Care and Hygiene

Poor contact lens hygiene is the main cause of contact lens-related eye infections, some of which can permanently damage vision. Follow these rules every time.

## Handling

- Wash your hands with soap and dry them with a lint-free towel before touching your lenses.
- Insert lenses before applying makeup, and remove them before removing makeup.

## Solution

- Use only fresh multipurpose solution or a hydrogen peroxide system recommended by your eye care professional.
- Never use water of any kind (tap, bottled, or distilled) or saliva to clean, rinse, or store lenses. Water can carry Acanthamoeba, an organism that causes a serious and painful eye infection.
- Rub and rinse your lenses with solution, even if the bottle says "no rub".
- Never top off old solution in the case. Throw it away and use fresh solution every time.
- Hydrogen peroxide solution must never go directly into the eye. It must be neutralized in its special case, usually for at least 6 hours, before the lenses are worn.

## Lens case

- After inserting your lenses, empty the case, rinse it with fresh solution (not water), and let it air-dry upside down with the caps off.
- Replace the case at least every 3 months.

## Wearing schedule

- Follow the replacement schedule for your lenses. Daily lenses are thrown away after one use. Monthly lenses are replaced one month after opening the pack, no matter how many days they were worn.
- Do not sleep in your lenses unless your eye care professional has prescribed lenses approved for overnight wear. Sleeping in lenses greatly increases the risk of infection.
- Most people should wear lenses for no more than about 8 to 12 hours a day, or as advised by their eye care professional.
- Remove lenses before swimming, showering, or using a hot tub. If lenses get wet, throw them away or disinfect them before wearing them again.
- Air conditioning and dust can make eyes feel dry. Use rewetting drops that are labelled as safe for contact lenses.
- Always keep an up-to-date pair of glasses as a backup.

## When to remove lenses immediately

Remove your lenses and see an eye doctor if you have eye redness, pain, sensitivity to light, blurred vision, or discharge. Do not wear lenses again until your eyes have been checked.
