# Eye Health FAQ

This information is general education, not medical advice. For symptoms or eye conditions, please see an ophthalmologist (dokter spesialis mata).

## Who does what?

A Refraksionis Optisien (RO), the optometrist-level professional in Indonesian optical stores, measures your vision and fits glasses and contact lenses. Eye diseases are diagnosed and treated by an ophthalmologist (dokter spesialis mata).

## How often should I have my eyes checked?

As general guidance: every 1 to 2 years for adults under 65, and every year from age 65. Children should have their eyes checked before starting school and then every year. People with diabetes should have a medical eye exam at least once a year. Your eye care professional may advise a different schedule.

## Does wearing glasses make my eyes weaker?

No. Glasses do not weaken the eyes or make a prescription increase. In children, myopia usually increases because the eyeball is still growing, whether or not they wear glasses. Wearing glasses that are weaker than needed does not slow myopia down.

## Does reading in dim light damage my eyes?

No. Reading in dim light can make your eyes feel tired, but it does not cause permanent damage.

## Will eating carrots improve my eyesight?

Carrots contain vitamin A, which the eyes need, and a balanced diet supports eye health. But eating carrots will not correct nearsightedness, farsightedness, or astigmatism.

## What are floaters?

Floaters are small specks or threads that drift across your vision. They are common and become more common with age. However, a sudden increase in floaters, especially with flashes of light or a shadow or curtain over part of your vision, can be a sign of retinal detachment and needs urgent care.

## What is presbyopia?

Presbyopia is the normal age-related loss of the ability to focus up close. It usually becomes noticeable around age 40, when people start holding their phone or book further away to read. It is corrected with reading glasses or with bifocal or progressive lenses.

## What is glaucoma?

Glaucoma is a group of diseases that damage the optic nerve. It usually has no symptoms in the early stages, so regular eye exams matter, especially after age 40 or if a family member has glaucoma.

## Warning signs: seek care immediately

Go to an eye doctor or emergency department right away if you have:

- Sudden loss of vision or sudden blurred vision in one or both eyes.
- A sudden increase in floaters, flashes of light, or a curtain over part of your vision.
- Severe eye pain, especially with headache, nausea, or seeing halos around lights.
- An eye injury.
- A chemical splash in the eye. Rinse the eye with clean water for at least 15 minutes first, then seek emergency care.
