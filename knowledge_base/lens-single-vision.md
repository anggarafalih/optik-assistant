# Single Vision Lenses

Single vision lenses have one prescription power across the whole lens. They correct vision at a single distance: either far away (for nearsightedness, also called myopia) or up close (for farsightedness, also called hyperopia, or for reading). They can also include correction for astigmatism.

## Who they are for

- People with myopia (a minus prescription) who need to see far away clearly.
- People with hyperopia (a plus prescription).
- People over about 40 with presbyopia who only need glasses for reading. Reading glasses are single vision lenses with plus power.
- Most children's first pair of glasses uses single vision lenses.

## Advantages

- The widest clear field of view, with no distortion zones at the sides.
- The easiest lens to adapt to, usually within a few days.
- The lowest cost of all lens designs.
- The fastest to produce. Common prescriptions are often available from stock.

## Limitations

Single vision lenses only correct one distance. Someone who needs correction for both distance and near vision will need two pairs of glasses, or a multifocal lens such as a bifocal or progressive lens.

## Options

Single vision lenses can be made in different materials (standard CR-39 plastic, polycarbonate, Trivex, or high-index) and combined with coatings and treatments such as anti-reflective coating, blue-light filtering, or photochromic (light-adaptive) lenses.
