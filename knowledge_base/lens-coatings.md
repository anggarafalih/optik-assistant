# Lens Coatings and Treatments

## Anti-reflective (AR) coating

An anti-reflective coating reduces reflections on both surfaces of the lens. It reduces glare from headlights when driving at night and from screens and office lights, and it makes the lenses look clearer in photos and conversations. Many AR coatings include a top layer that repels water (hydrophobic) and oil (oleophobic), which makes the lenses easier to clean.

## Scratch-resistant hard coat

Most plastic lenses come with a hard coat that makes the surface more resistant to scratches. No lens is scratch-proof: wiping a dry, dusty lens or cleaning with tissue can still scratch it.

## UV protection

Polycarbonate, Trivex, and high-index lenses block UV light by themselves. Standard CR-39 plastic lenses need an added UV coating.

## Tints

Lenses can be tinted in a fixed colour, either evenly or as a gradient (darker at the top, lighter at the bottom). A light tint is a style choice. A dark tint turns the glasses into prescription sunglasses.

## Polarized lenses

Polarized lenses block horizontally reflected glare from water, wet roads, and car bonnets. They are used for sunglasses, especially for driving and fishing.

## Mirror coating

A mirror coating is a reflective layer on the front of sunglass lenses. It reduces the light reaching the eye and is mostly a style choice.

## Heat damages coatings

Coatings can develop a network of fine cracks, called crazing, when exposed to heat. Do not leave glasses on a car dashboard in the sun, near a stove, or in a sauna, and do not wash them with hot water. Heat damage to coatings is not a manufacturing defect.
