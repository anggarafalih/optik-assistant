# Frame Size and Fit Guide

## Reading the frame measurements

Most frames have three numbers printed on the inside of a temple arm, for example **52□18 140**:

- **52** is the lens width in millimetres.
- **18** is the bridge width (the distance between the two lenses) in millimetres.
- **140** is the temple (arm) length in millimetres.

Some frames also print the lens height. If your current glasses fit well, these numbers are a good starting point for choosing a new frame.

## Signs of a good fit

- Your eyes are roughly centred horizontally in each lens.
- The frame is about as wide as your face at the temples, and does not pinch or leave marks.
- The bridge or nose pads sit comfortably on your nose, and the frame does not rest on your cheeks.
- The top of the frame roughly follows your eyebrow line.
- The temple arms reach over your ears without pressing, and the glasses do not slide down when you look at the floor.

## Low nose bridge (Asian fit)

Many customers in Indonesia and across Asia have a lower nose bridge. Frames with adjustable nose pads, or "Asian fit" / "low bridge fit" plastic frames with a higher, deeper bridge, keep the frame from sliding down or resting on the cheeks.

## Face shape suggestions

- Round face: angular or rectangular frames add definition.
- Square face: round or oval frames soften strong lines.
- Oval face: most frame shapes suit an oval face.
- Heart-shaped face: bottom-heavy, light, or rimless frames balance a wider forehead.

These are only suggestions. The best frame is one that fits well and that you like.

## Special cases

- Strong prescriptions: smaller, rounder frames keep lenses thinner and lighter.
- Progressive lenses: the frame needs enough lens height for all the zones.
- Frames can be adjusted by our staff to improve the fit.
